# Equivariant affine charts for D(M) without stacks: AHR Cor. 10.2, special case

Target (AHR, arXiv:1912.06162, Cor. 10.2, checked in that text). M f.g. abelian, G = D(M), S affine of finite type over Z, X separated algebraic space of finite type over S with G-action, x closed point. Find U affine and (U,u) -> (X,x) étale, G-equivariant, with κ(u) = κ(x), G_u = G_x.

## 0. Result in one paragraph

**Proved** (no stacks, no coherent completeness, no Tannaka, no Artin approximation):

- Theorem A. For any X as above, the target reduces to (⋆): a split torus T, a rational fixed point. All of the finite part (including μ_p) and all non-fixed points are handled for algebraic spaces.
- Theorem B. (⋆) reduces, by induction on closed subspaces and pinching, to the normal case (N).
- (N) holds when the normalization is a scheme near the fibre, by Sumihiro's theorem. **So the target is proved for all schemes X, including non-normal ones, and for all spaces whose relevant normalizations are schemes (e.g. pinchings of schemes that are not schemes).**

**Not proved: (N) for a normal algebraic space that is not a scheme at x.** This is the one gap, see §5. I have no proof of the target for general algebraic spaces. The input Sumihiro [Equivariant completion II, Cor. 3.11] is cited through Algebraic Magnetism (AM) Rem. 7.4 and Drinfeld Def. 1.4.5; I did not read Sumihiro himself, and the proof of the theorem for schemes depends on it. Nothing here has been refereed.

(⋆) Let T = D(Z^r)_S. Let x be a closed point of X, fixed by T, with κ(x) = κ(s), where s is its image in S. Then an affine T-equivariant étale chart at x exists with κ(u) = κ(x).

For T a torus, u is automatically fixed (AM Prop. 6.3; in the construction below it is also visible directly).

Notation: for Z ⊂ M, D(M/Z) ⊂ D(M) is a closed subgroup with quotient D(Z). For V = Spec B with D(M/Z)-action, B is M/Z-graded.

## 1. Preliminary reductions and lemmas

**1.1 Rational point.** S is of finite type over Z, so κ(x) and κ(s) are finite fields and κ(x)/κ(s) is separable. Choose S' -> S étale, S' affine, with s' over s, κ(s') = κ(x). Then x gives a rational closed point x' of X' = X ×_S S'. A chart at x' composed with X' -> X is a chart at x with the same residue field and stabilizer. Henceforth k := κ(x) = κ(s). (This is the place where "finite type over Z" is really used; for non-separable κ(x)/κ(s) it would need replacing.)

**1.2 Stabilizer is constant.** G_x is a closed subgroup of G_k (X separated) so G_x = D(M/Z_0)_k for a subgroup Z_0 ⊂ M (SGA3 VIII 3.2 / Cartier duality). Put H = D(M/Z_0)_S. The orbit map gives a monomorphism O := G_k/H_k = D(Z_0)_k -> X.

**1.3 Shrinking Lemma.** Let Y = Spec A with G-action, Ω ⊂ Y a G-stable open, O ⊂ Y a closed G-stable subscheme contained in Ω. Then there is f ∈ A_0 with O ⊂ D(f) ⊂ Ω.

Proof. Let C = Y \ Ω, I its radical ideal. The set C is stable, so for g ∈ I the coaction Σ g_m X^m is nilpotent modulo I·A[M]. Hence for some N all homogeneous components of g^N lie in I. Let J be generated by these components as g runs through generators of I. J is homogeneous and V(J) = C (non-trivial when G is not reduced: the radical of a homogeneous ideal need not be homogeneous, e.g. T-1 in k[T]/(T^p-1)). I_O + J = A with both homogeneous, so 1 = i + j in degree 0, with j ∈ J_0. Take f = j. ∎

**1.4 Cofinality Lemma.** A f.g. M-graded noetherian with M = Z^r, m ⊂ A a homogeneous maximal ideal. Then m^ℓ ∩ A_δ ⊂ m_0^{f(ℓ)} A_δ with f(ℓ) -> ∞, for each δ.

Proof. Choose homogeneous generators e_1..e_N of m with degrees d_j. Multi-indices ν with Σ ν_j d_j = δ are finitely many ν_0 plus nonnegative combinations of finitely many generators γ_k of the monoid of solutions with δ = 0 (Dickson/Gordan). A long ν uses many γ_k, each a monomial in m_0. ∎

So lim (A/m^n)_δ is the m_0-adic completion of the finite A_0-module A_δ (A_0 f.g. and A_δ finite over A_0, also Gordan).

**1.5 Luna Lemma (torus case, elementary).** Let C -> B be a T-equivariant map of f.g. Z^r-graded algebras, étale at r ∈ Spec B, with r, p = image closed fixed and κ(r) = κ(p). Then C_0 -> B_0 is étale at r̄ and C ⊗_{C_0} B_0 -> B becomes an isomorphism after inverting some s ∈ B_0 with s(r) ≠ 0.

Proof. C/m_p^n = B/m_r^n as graded rings. By 1.4, this identifies completions of C_0 and B_0, and of C_δ and B_δ for every δ. So C_0 -> B_0 is étale at r̄ (EGA IV 17.6.3) and the map is an isomorphism on each graded piece at the local ring of r̄. Finitely many degrees generate B over the image, and the kernel is a f.g. graded ideal, so finitely many degrees suffice and one s works. ∎

## 2. Finite step (idea (a), corrected)

**Prop 2.1 (Coinduction).** Let H' = D(M/Z_1) with Z_1 finite, f : V -> X étale H'-equivariant, V affine. Let F be the functor of pairs (p ∈ X(T), φ : G_T -> V) with φ H'-equivariant and f∘φ(g) = g·p. Then F is an affine scheme, F -> X is étale and G-equivariant for (γ·φ)(g) = φ(gγ).

Proof. φ corresponds to a section s(g) = [g, φ(g^{-1})] of Spec E -> Ḡ, with Ḡ = D(Z_1), E = ⊕_{m∈M} B_{[m]} X^m ⊂ B[M]; p is determined by φ. Ḡ is finite free over S, Spec E is of finite type over it, so Res_{Ḡ/S}(Spec E) is an affine scheme (write out coordinates in a basis of O_S[Z_1]). The condition f∘φ(g) = g·f(φ(e)) compares two maps G_R -> X, H'-equivariant. Since X is separated, the equalizer is closed and H'-stable, so it descends to closed E' ⊂ Ḡ_R. The locus where E' = Ḡ_R is closed in R as Ḡ is finite free. Thus F is closed in an affine scheme. Formally étale: on a thickening T_0 ⊂ T, φ_0 lifts uniquely along V -> X, equivariance follows from uniqueness. Finite presentation holds as F is of finite type. ∎

Here separatedness is really used. It is the only use of X separated beyond 1.2. Without it, F is not closed in the Weil restriction.

**Prop 2.2 (points).** Keep x rational with stabilizer H.

(i) If x is G-fixed, H' ⊂ G, and v ∈ V is a rational H'-fixed point over x, then φ_0 := const v defines u ∈ F(k) with G_u = G. For split G = G_m^r × D(T) and H' = G_m^r: (⋆) implies the fixed-point case for G.

(ii) If H ⊂ H', H'/H is a torus, and for each closed point y of X in the G-orbit of x we have an H'-chart (V_y, v_y) with κ(v_y) = κ(y), stabilizer H, then V = ⊔ V_y carries an H'-equivariant lift φ_0 : O -> V of O -> X, giving u ∈ F(k) with κ(u) = k, G_u = H.

Proof of (ii). The fibre O_i of O -> Ḡ_k at a closed point ḡ_i with residue field k_i has reduced part an H'/H-torsor over k_i, trivial by Hilbert 90: pick g_i and put y_i = g_i x. O -> X is a monomorphism, so κ(y_i) = k_i. Define on the reduced part h ↦ h·v_{y_i}. Lift over the nilpotent thickening uniquely. Stabilizer: φ_0 factors through G/H so H ⊂ G_u, and G_u ⊂ G_x = H since F -> X is equivariant. ∎

**μ_p test.** X = A^1 in char p, μ_p with weight 1, V : y^p - y = x (étale at 0, not equivariant). F = V since lifts along infinitesimal μ_p are unique. The induced Z/p-grading: x in degree 1, w := y + x in degree 0 (w^p - w = x^p). The method works for non-étale G and gives gradings that no choice of V can extend to Z (see §6).

**Free μ_p test.** X = μ_p, G = μ_p, x = 1: the point has trivial stabilizer, F = X. Fine; a slice argument is not available here, since G×W -> X is flat, not smooth. That is why finite parts must go through Prop 2.1 and not slices.

## 3. Slice step (torus quotient)

**Prop 3.1.** Suppose G/H = D(Z_0) with Z_0 free of rank d, x rational with stabilizer H, and (V,v) -> (X,x) an affine H-chart, v rational and H-fixed. Then a G-chart at x with stabilizer H exists.

Proof. Let V = Spec B, B being M/Z_0-graded. Y := G ×^H V = Spec ⊕_m B_{[m]} X^m, affine, G×V -> Y an H-torsor (each degree contains the unit X^m). G×^H X = G/H × X by (g,p) ↦ (gH, gp), so Y -> G/H × X is étale and Y -> X is smooth of relative dimension d, with y = [e,v].

The orbit map gives T_e(G/H) -> T_x X = T_v V injective (O -> X is mono, thus unramified), landing in the H-fixed part (H acts trivially on O), which is dual to (m_v/m_v²)_0. As m_v is homogeneous, choose a_1..a_d ∈ B_0 with a_i(v) = 1 and da_i restricting to a dual basis on T_e(G/H). They are G-invariant functions on Y, c_i([g,w]) = a_i(w).

The tangent space at y of the fibre of Y -> X over x is {(ξ, -ξ·x)}, on which dc_i(ξ, -ξ x) = -da_i(ξ x). So (pr_X, c) : Y -> X × A^d is étale at y. Therefore Y' := V(c_i - 1) = G ×^H W', with W' = V(a_i - 1) ⊂ V H-stable, is étale over X at y.

The étale locus Ω ⊂ Y' is open and G-stable. The orbit G·y = G/H × {v} is closed in Y' (its preimage G×{v} in G×W' is closed). Apply 1.3: a G-stable affine D(f) ⊂ Ω containing y. Also κ(y) = k, G_y = H. ∎

The a_i's existence needs no reductivity beyond the grading.

## 4. Theorem A and Theorem B

**Theorem A.** If (⋆) holds for all split subtori and all closed T-fixed rational points of all X' = X×_S S', S' -> S affine étale, then the target holds for (X,x).

Proof. After 1.1, Z_0 ⊂ M with H = D(M/Z_0). Let H = G_m^a × D(T') (choice of splitting). By (⋆) and 2.2(i), get an H-chart at each closed point y of the G-orbit, after base change 1.1 for non-rational y. Let Z_1 = tors(Z_0), H' = D(M/Z_1), H'/H = D(Z_0/Z_1) a torus. Prop 3.1 gives H'-charts. G/H' = D(Z_1) is finite. Props 2.1, 2.2(ii) give a G-chart. ∎

**Theorem B.** Assume (N): for each reduced normal X' with T-action in the dévissage, and each finite set of closed points x_i' fixed by T, there are T-stable affine étale charts (W_i, w_i) -> (X', x_i') with κ(w_i) = κ(x_i'). Then (⋆) holds for X.

(N) is known when X' is a scheme: T-stable affine open neighbourhoods, Sumihiro via AM Rem. 7.4.

Proof. Noetherian induction on T-stable closed subspaces.

*Nilpotents.* A chart for X_red (T smooth, so X_red stable) deforms uniquely to an étale U -> X; U is affine because U_red is, and T acts by uniqueness of lifts.

*Reduced X.* ν : X' -> X normalization, finite (S excellent), T-equivariant since normalization commutes with smooth base change T×X -> X. Conductor D ⊂ X, D' = ν^{-1}(D), T-stable, D nowhere dense. Étale locally on X the square is a Milnor square R = R' ×_{R'/I} R/I, directly from the definition of the conductor. If x ∉ D apply (N) on X \ D. Otherwise induction gives (U_D, u) -> (D, x), u rational and fixed.

Points x_i' of ν^{-1}(x) are all fixed (T is connected, the fibre is finite) with residue fields k_i. Put Q = U_D ×_D D', with a point q_i over each x_i'; (N) gives W_i, P_i = W_i ×_{X'} D' with point p_i; R_i = P_i ×_{D'} Q with k_i-point r_i. Write (-)//T for Spec of the degree-0 subring. By 1.5 applied to R_i -> P_i and R_i -> Q at r_i: near r_i,

    P_i ×_{P_i//T} Ē_i = R_i = Q ×_{Q//T} Ē_i,   Ē_i := R_i//T étale over both quotients.

Q -> U_D is finite, so Q//T -> U_D//T is finite. Every point of Q//T over ū lies under a closed orbit over the unique closed orbit u of the fibre, hence equals some q̄_i. After passing to the henselization at ū, Q//T splits into local pieces at the q̄_i, and each piece maps to Ē_i since Ē_i -> Q//T is étale with trivial residue extension at r̄_i. By a limit argument, there is an étale (B,b) -> (U_D//T, ū), κ(b) = k, with

    Q//T ×_{U_D//T} B = ⊔ Ξ_i,  Ξ_i -> Ē_i.

Shrink B (removing images of closed sets under the finite map) so that isomorphisms above hold over all Ξ_i.

(A/I)_0 = A_0/I_0 by exactness, so P_i//T is closed in W_i//T. Stacks 04D1 lifts Ξ_i -> P_i//T to an étale E_i -> W_i//T, with E_i ×_{W_i//T} P_i//T = Ξ_i. Set W_1 = ⊔ W_i ×_{W_i//T} E_i (T-equivariant étale over X', affine), U_B = U_D ×_{U_D//T} B. Then

    W_1 ×_{X'} D' ≅ ⊔ P_i ×_{P_i//T} Ξ_i ≅ Q ×_{U_D//T} B = U_B ×_D D'.

Call this Z_1. Z_1 -> W_1 is a closed immersion, Z_1 -> U_B finite. Let U = Spec(O(U_B) ×_{O(Z_1)} O(W_1)), an affine scheme, graded, of finite type (Stacks 15.5.1), and a pushout in schemes (Stacks 37.67.3) and in algebraic spaces (Stacks 81.7.1, tag 0GG2). Thus U_B -> D -> X and W_1 -> X' -> X induce U -> X; T×U is again the pushout (flat), so U -> X is equivariant.

*Étale.* Take Spec R -> X étale surjective. Forming the pullbacks of W_1, U_B, Z_1 and pushing out gives Ü with Ü -> Spec R étale by Stacks 37.67.7 (étale version; or 15.6.8, 15.7 tag 0D2K for flatness, and fibres via R -> R' surjective on spectra) and Ü -> U étale surjective by the same lemma. Being étale is local on the source, so U -> X is étale at all points.

U_B -> U is a closed immersion, so u has κ(u) = k and is fixed. ∎

**Corollary.** The target holds for all schemes X, and for all spaces X such that the normalization of each reduced T-stable closed subspace through x is a scheme near the fibre of x.

**Nodal cubic test.** X^ν = P^1, D = node, D' = {0,∞}. W_0, W_∞ = A^1 with weights ±1. U = pinching = Spec k[a,b]/(ab) -> X étale, equivariant. This is the expected chart, and Sumihiro fails on X. The method treats exactly the case Sumihiro misses.

## 5. The gap: (N) for normal non-schemes

(N) asks: X normal, separated, T acting, x fixed, X not a scheme at x. Here is everything I tried.

- **Zariski methods are impossible.** A T-linearized L with sections that are quasi-finite at x would map a T-stable open to an affine and make it a scheme by Zariski's main theorem, contradicting non-schematicity. So Sumihiro's proof cannot be transported.
- **Galois cover, then Sumihiro.** With V -> X étale and L the Galois closure of K(V)/K(X), the normalization Y of X in L is a scheme near the fibre (Knutson). With T acting on Y, the quotient of a T×Γ_1-stable affine by the decomposition group Γ_1 would be a chart. The problem: T does not act on Y, not even up to isogeny. For X = A^1 (or G_m) in char p and V : y^p - y = x, the pullback a^*V has equation y^p - y = tx, which is not isotrivial in t. Wild covers really obstruct this, and the cover must be chosen T-homogeneous, which is the original problem.
- **What might work, unproved.** Let C = ⊕_δ lim (O/m^n)_δ be the graded completion of X at x, and Â its usual completion. For X normal, the idea is: T-semi-invariant fractions suggest K(X) ⊂ Frac C, so L := alg. closure of K(X) in Frac C is T-homogeneous and L ⊂ Frac O^h. For L_1 ⊂ L finite, the normalization Y_1 of X in L_1 has a T-action and is étale at the point over x. Remaining claim: for L_1 large, Y_1 is a scheme at that point. In the AS example K(V) ⊄ L, so V cannot simply be included; I have no argument that L is large enough, and K(X) ⊂ Frac C itself is verified only in factorial Â. **Open.**

## 6. Your four ideas, and precise failures of simple approaches

(a) Finite G by norm/Weil restriction: **works**, §2, in fact as coinduction, and gives the non-étale case. Note: translates do not suffice ("intersection of translates" is meaningless for non-reduced G), the functor F is the right form. Points of the orbit other than x need their own charts when H' is not contained in H, 2.2(ii).

(b) G_m by V ×_X (G_m×V): this gives a groupoid, not an action. Drinfeld (arXiv:1308.2604, read §§0.4, 1.2, 1.4, 4) does not construct charts. He proves X^+ -> X^0 is affine directly, by a jet algebra A^+ (Prop 4.4.4); with X separated, X^+ -> X̂^+ × X is a closed embedding (Rem. 4.5.4). That handles the **contracting case only**: if X = X^+ (over a field, his setting), then X = Spec_{X^0} A^+, and any étale affine V^0 -> X^0 with the trivial action gives a chart by pullback. Off X^+ ∪ X^- (e.g. xy = c in A^2 with weights ±1) no flow reaches x. His Lemma 4.5.3 uses Moret-Bailly gluing (Thm 4.6.1), and all of this concerns maps from A^1 only.

Taking μ_n-charts F_n for large n to approximate T: fails. On X = A^1, F_n is a fibre product of translates of V with growing degree, and in the AS example never T-homogeneous, for all n.

(c) Induction on G_m^r: **not needed** given §4; (⋆) is treated for all r at once. Induction via quotient by the first G_m would require a chart for one G_m first and then the same problem again, so nothing is gained.

(d) Deforming a chart of X^0 (AM Thm 3.20, Prop 5.1, §11), and algebraizing. X_n := n-th neighbourhood of x is T-stable affine, giving C, Â, Spec Â -> X. Then: G × Spec Â -> Spec C is an fpqc cover, since all orbit closures in Spec C contain x, and (g,a) ↦ g·ϕ(a) to X should descend. The cocycle condition compares two maps on R_0 = (G×Spec Â) ×_{Spec C} Spec Â, which agree on all thickenings of the fixed part. **This fails to conclude:** R_0 is not noetherian and not separated. Already for C = k[t], Â = k[[t]], a ⊗ 1 - 1 ⊗ a lies in ∩ t^n (Â ⊗_C Â) for all a ∈ k[[t]]. That is the exact point where AHR use Tannaka duality. Henselization plus limit does not avoid it, because C_0^h and a finite-type model are only useful once Spec C -> X exists: equivariant Artin approximation applies to R ↦ Hom_T(Spec C ⊗_{C_0} R, X), which starts from this map. Moret-Bailly gluing (Drinfeld Thm 4.6.1) replaces the map on Spec C by the map on Spec C \ V(I), available for A^1 (it is the orbit) and unavailable in general. Prop 5.1 and §11 enter as consistency, but give no construction: they say what a chart must do on attractors.

The dévissage of §4 is what escapes this, by using Sumihiro at the normal stage and Luna's lemma, which needs only affine inputs.

**Non-separated X.** Separatedness enters at 1.2 and 2.1. Prop 3.1, Luna, pinching do not use it beyond statements. AHR Cor. 10.2 covers quasi-separated X; this approach does not.

## 7. Lean feasibility

Static grep in `.lake/packages` (Mathlib AlgebraicGeometry, RingTheory; Stacks); no build.

- Available: algebraic spaces (`StacksFrontier/AlgebraicGeometry/Spaces/*`, atlases, étale charts, affine morphisms), AM attractors in this repo, étale ring maps (Mathlib).
- Not found: Weil restriction, torsors or quotients by D(M) (no hit for "Torsor", "Diagonalizable"), henselization construction, pinching, Sumihiro. "normalization" has 27 file hits; I did not open them, but the three listed paths (Epi, descent, groupoids) do not suggest normalization of spaces, so it should not be assumed.

Honest plan: formalize Theorem A with (⋆) as a hypothesis, and Theorem B with (N) as a hypothesis. Unconditional Lean formalization of the target is out of reach, and in any case the mathematics of (N) is open here.
